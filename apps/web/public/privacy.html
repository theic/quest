Privacy policy

Last updated 20 November 2026

Quest finds the people who want what you built and writes to them with you, from your own Chrome. You can run it yourself, on your own computer, or use the one we host. This page says what each keeps, what Quest does in your Chrome, who helps us run it, and what you can ask of us.

Messaging Quest, based in Portugal, is the data controller for everything described here. Write to hello@messaging.quest with any question or request.

If you run Quest yourself

Quest is open source (AGPL-3.0). Run on your own computer, it keeps your chat, your brief, its drafts, the people you talk with and everything in sections 1 and 2 below in a folder on that computer (~/.quest), and thinks with the Claude or ChatGPT subscription you sign in to there, under that provider's own terms. We never see any of it, with one exception: the public people it finds (section 3), which it sends to the shared list of people we keep, with an anonymous key it makes for itself. Telegram is used only if you give it your own bot.

1. What we keep about you (hosted Quest)

  • Your chat. Every message between you and Quest, both ways, in the extension's side panel or in Telegram if you link it (then also your Telegram chat id, and the name, username and language Telegram shares with the bot). Files you send are read as text and kept with the chat.
  • What Quest learns to do the work. The brief about what you offer, its plan, the notes it keeps (what you decided, what worked, facts about you), your answers to the first-day questions, your time zone and your settings.
  • The work. The places and people it found, the drafts it wrote, what went up and where, the answers it watched for, the results you or it recorded, and a line for every step it and its team took (kept 14 days). We also keep what each AI call cost, with no message content (kept 30 days).
  • Your Chrome connection. A hash of the extension's token, the device name it reports (such as "Chrome on Windows") and when it was last seen.

Legal basis: performing our agreement with you. We do not sell your data or share it for anyone's advertising.

2. What Quest does in your Chrome

The Messaging Quest extension lets Quest read the web in your own Chrome, in a tab group of its own that closes when it is done, in the profile where you installed it, with your logins. It opens only public addresses, at a reader's pace, and at most 400 pages a day. What it reads (page text, posts, comments and their links) comes to us so Quest can do the work. Your passwords and cookies stay in Chrome: the extension never sends them to us.

  • Posting and messages. Quest drafts replies, posts and messages and sends them from your own accounts in your Chrome. It asks before it sends until you have approved a few of a kind on a platform; words that name your product, private messages and profile changes always wait for you. Say "ask me first" at any time and it will ask before each one.
  • Quest's tab in your chat. When you ask to see a page, Quest can send you a picture of its own tab (never one of your tabs, and never a login, mail or bank page). We do not keep these pictures.
  • Visual controls. When a site hides its controls, Quest can look at a picture of the tab it is working in and click or type there. The pictures go to the AI model so it can see the page; they never include your other tabs, and we do not keep them. It is on unless you turn it off with its switch in the extension.
  • Keeping awake. While connected, the extension asks the computer not to sleep. Its switch in the extension turns that off.

3. The people Quest finds, and the shared list

To find people who want what you built, Quest reads public posts. Every Quest, hosted or run on someone's own computer, adds the public people it finds to one shared list we keep, and reads everyone's recent finds back, so a maker found by one meets someone who asked for their thing on another. The list holds only what is public: a person's handle on a site, the name, profile and site they show there, what they made or asked for in their own post, and that post's address. Never a customer's words, drafts, chat or who they are. Legal basis: the legitimate interest, ours and our customers', in introducing people who asked in public. Someone who tells any Quest to leave them alone is taken off the list for every Quest, and anyone can ask us at hello@messaging.quest to remove what we hold about them.

4. If you signed up for our earlier web app

From July to September 2026 Messaging Quest was a web app you signed up for with your email. If you did, we keep your account: your email, whether you confirmed it, the site you gave us and your choice about product news. We do not email you. What that app kept besides (projects, campaigns, the contacts and posts it found) is archived and no longer used. Ask us and we delete your account and everything with it.

6. Outside the EU

Our database and Quest itself run in the EU. OpenRouter is in the United States, and the model providers it reaches may process data in the United States or China. What goes to them is what Quest needs for a step of the work: your messages, the brief and the pages it read.

7. How long we keep it

We keep your chat and Quest's work while you use Quest. The step log is kept 14 days, the cost records 30 days, and a finished task's working files about an hour. Ask us and we delete your account and everything Quest kept for you.

8. Your rights

You can ask to see what we hold about you, have it corrected or deleted, receive a copy, restrict how we use it, or object to a use based on legitimate interest. Write to hello@messaging.quest. You can also complain to Portugal's data protection authority, the CNPD (cnpd.pt), or to the authority where you live.

9. This website

This website sets no cookies. To see which parts of it work, it counts visits with PostHog: the page, the site you came from, the links and buttons you click, how far you read, your browser and device type, and your rough location, which PostHog works out from your IP address. Nothing is stored on your device for this, so we can't link one visit to the next, and we never connect it to your Quest account. If you pick a language, your browser remembers it in its own storage. Vercel, which hosts the site, keeps standard request logs, such as your IP address, to serve and protect it. The video on the home page and the fonts are served from the site itself, like its pages.

10. Children

Quest is not for anyone under 16.

11. Changes

When this policy changes, the date at the top changes too. When a change affects what we keep or who sees it, we tell you in the chat.
